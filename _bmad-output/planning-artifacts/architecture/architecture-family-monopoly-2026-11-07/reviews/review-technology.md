# Technology and source reconciliation review

Verdict: concerns; one renderer-scope decision needs explicit owner agreement and several upstream updates need tracking.

1. **High — renderer decision broadened:** AD-5 binds all board/action clients to Phaser, but ADR-007 accepts Phaser for the local game table and EPIC-003 says to reuse the local presentation where practical. This makes a new cross-device technology decision, not a direct adoption of ADR-007. Obtain the owner's choice or scope the binding to local play and leave private-client renderer choice open.
2. **Medium — changed recovery policy has not landed upstream:** AD-6 records the owner's new device-switch recovery requirement and no host reset. ADR-003 and EPIC-003 currently defer cross-device recovery, and EPIC-004 requires lifecycle and security design. This is not a contradiction if clearly marked future, but the spine should identify the required upstream update before implementation and distinguish current behavior. Offer to update requirements, scenarios and planning artifacts as a separately approved slice.
3. **Medium — single instance is a recommendation, not an adopted decision:** The spine correctly calls this a working direction; the owner specified four people and acceptable interruptions rather than selecting deployment topology. Preserve that qualification and avoid implying this architecture alone approves a hosting design.

Evidence: `package.json` pins Bun 1.4.4 and Phaser 3.90.0, consistent with the spine and ADR-005/007. Official [Bun documentation](https://bun.com/docs) verifies the runtime/toolkit, and official [Phaser documentation](https://docs.phaser.io/) verifies 2D browser support with WebGL and Canvas. The sources do not establish that Phaser 3.90.0 is current; the spine accurately identifies it as an existing pin rather than latest. EPIC-003 identifies private authored-profile routing as future work and explicitly isolates seat credentials; the structural seed accurately reflects that boundary. EPIC-004 keeps hosting authorization, retention, threat model and production readiness open.
